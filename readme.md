💥1 Overview
Over the past seven years, advancements in materials, electronics, sensors, and batteries have propelled the development of micro unmanned aerial vehicles (MAVs), with lengths ranging from 0.1-0.5 meters to 0.1-0.5 kilograms. Some groups have established and analyzed MAVs within a 10 cm range. The smallest among them is Picoflyer, with a propeller diameter of 60 mm and a mass of 3.3 grams. MAV platforms within a 50 cm range are more common, with some groups having built and flown systems of this size. In fact, there are several commercial RC helicopters and research-grade helicopters within this size range. This paper presents the challenges faced by the development of autonomous micro drones. In this project, we introduce the use of four-rotor modeling in the GRASP multipurpose micro aircraft testbed to support research on coordinated dynamic flight of micro aircraft. This article is based on a complete reference [10], which describes the modeling, control, and integration methods for existing four-rotor systems. For detailed explanations, see section four.
